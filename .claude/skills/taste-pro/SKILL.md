# Taste Pro

Act as the visual design lead. Make deliberate choices rooted in the product and audience, not defaults from a generic app template.

## Art direction

1. Identify one memorable visual idea and let the rest of the interface support it. State the palette, type roles, layout principle, and use of imagery briefly before building.
2. Ground the direction in the product’s real subject, audience, language, and place. Keep the design polished, coherent, and original. Use references for clarity only; do not copy another product’s visual identity.
3. Use visual hierarchy to carry information. Keep decorative labels, repeated cards, gradients, borders, and motion only when they serve a clear purpose.
4. Choose Arabic and Latin type that remain legible together. Check Arabic shaping, line breaks, punctuation, dates, numerals, and RTL alignment in the rendered interface.
5. Use accessible contrast, visible focus, responsive composition, and reduced-motion behavior as part of the visual system.
6. Review screenshots at desktop and mobile sizes. Remove one unnecessary visual element, then fix any remaining issues found by the design critique and accessibility review.

## KASHTA visual context

Make the visual identity feel specifically Saudi and Arabic-first, centered on a starry desert night and the warmth of a kashta. A night sky, dunes, a small campsite, and restrained hospitality cues can support the story. Najdi forms, coffee, or Sadu-inspired geometry may be used sparingly and accurately; avoid generic Gulf clichés or turning cultural references into decoration on every screen.

Keep the stars and campsite visible in the opening experience while preserving readable recommendations and controls. Use the existing KASHTA illustration work where it fits. Clearly distinguish illustrative scenes from actual places and live forecast data. Do not use a stock photo as if it depicts a recommended destination.

Inspect `.taste-skill/direction.json` and existing screenshots when present. Treat generated directions as references, not constraints. In particular, do not follow a fixed photo-led split-screen direction if it conflicts with KASHTA’s night-sky, bilingual product experience.
